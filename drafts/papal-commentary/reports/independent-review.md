## Source contexts actually read

Eleven papal documents, with these complete supporting sections and adjoining argument:

- Benedict XVI, **Deus Caritas Est 5–7 and 16–18**: embodied love, purification, giving/receiving, and love of neighbour opening recognition of God.
- Benedict XVI, **Sacramentum Caritatis 66 and 70–71**: reception and adoration; Eucharistic transformation of ordinary embodied existence.
- Benedict XVI, **Verbum Domini 54–55**: John 6 and Emmaus explicitly read together; the mutual illumination of Scripture and Eucharist.
- Benedict XVI, **Spe Salvi 7 and 42–48**: present participation in hope, resurrection and justice for victims, purification and prayer for the dead.
- Benedict XVI, **Caritas in Veritate 2–3 and 6**: love and truth, charity and what justice already owes another person.
- John Paul II, **Fides et Ratio 20–23**: faith's relation to reason and the challenge of the Cross to closed systems.
- John Paul II, **Veritatis Splendor 16–24**: commandments, free response, personal discipleship and enabling grace.
- John Paul II, **Salvifici Doloris 24 and 27–29**: the completeness of redemption, participation by those who suffer, and indispensable organised care joined to personal compassion.
- John Paul II, **Dives in Misericordia 5–6 and 14**, including footnotes 120–122: sonship and restored dignity; the giver's reception of mercy; the explicit Matthew 5/Matthew 25 connection.
- John Paul II, **Redemptor Hominis 10**: the whole section on human dignity and self-understanding in encounter with Christ.
- John Paul II, **Ecclesia de Eucharistia 18–20**: bodily resurrection, heavenly communion, and responsibility in the present world.

Retained source contexts were also checked: CCC 146; 458; 618; 694; 1030–1032; 1362–1365; 1439; 1611; 1931–1933; 2100; 2488–2492; Chrysostom's opening argument and relevant paragraph in Homily 4 on 1 Corinthians; Augustine's full section 12 and conclusion in Homily 5 on 1 John. The additions have distinct purposes:

- Adoration remains distinct from the neighbouring real-presence and Eucharistic-unity notes.
- Emmaus now has one new connection rather than both a rewritten local note and a duplicating link.
- 1 John 4:20 concerns how serving changes our recognition of God; the mercy connection concerns the recipient's equal dignity and the giver also receiving. These are related but meaningfully different.
- 1 John 3:18's justice qualification overlaps the broad moral principle in Amos 2:6, but applies it directly to John's charity command while preserving Augustine's accessible beginning. It does not add another standalone note on commerce.
- The judgement link adds the resurrection's answer to past victims, extending the existing Revelation martyr note and enemy-love link without merely restating either.
- Romans 12:1 concerns the Eucharistic form of daily life; 1 Corinthians 11:26 concerns the future resurrection's claim upon present responsibility. The complementary emphases are preserved.

## Important distinctions preserved

**Theological proposal versus doctrine:** note 891 explicitly says Benedict explores a theological reading of purifying fire as encounter with Christ. It does not report this particular image as a defined account of the mechanics or duration of Purgatory. The doctrinal distinction between purification of those saved and condemnation remains explicit.

**Faith and language:** Hebrews 11:1 does not turn Benedict's discussion of hypostasis into an uncontested lexical correction of the app's translation. Faith is present participation in God's promised life, not the power to manufacture a desired outcome.

**Suffering:** the Colossians revision retains the completeness of Christ's sacrifice, the need to relieve suffering, and the dignity of people unable to display spiritual productivity. Neither pain's intensity nor forced endurance becomes a measure of holiness. The Samaritan revision respects organised healthcare rather than opposing institutions to compassion.

**Mercy and justice:** the resurrection link does not erase victims' injuries. The reciprocal-mercy link makes no demand for gratitude or repayment from recipients. The prodigal's restored dignity remains compatible with truthful conversion.

**Sacramental participation:** the Eucharistic revisions retain the unique accomplished sacrifice; they do not imply another crucifixion. New references to adoration, transformation and resurrection are directly supported by the cited sections.

**Attribution and prose:** distinctive papal readings are named, with original editorial wording rather than invented quotations or imitation of papal authorship. The final notes remain concise, ranging from 85 to 99 words before citations.
