## Selections and biblical context

- **891, 1 Corinthians 3:13**: retains work built on Christ, the builder saved, and purification distinct from condemnation. Adds Benedict's explicitly attributed exploration of Christ's encounter as the purifying fire. This is presented as a theological reading, not a newly defined physical account or measurable duration. Read the complete chapter, including the preceding division between Paul and Apollos and the following community-temple image.
- **994, Hebrews 11:1**: keeps Abraham, God's trustworthy promise, and faith persisting through suffering. Adds §7's present beginning of the future hoped for. Does not settle the disputed translation of hypostasis or import Benedict's historical criticism of particular exegesis. Read Hebrews 11:1–16 and 32–40; these include both apparent successes and faithful deaths before fulfilment.
- **935, Ephesians 4:15**: preserves truthful, discreet, responsible communication while adding that love needs an account of the real human good, rather than merely sincere feeling. Read Ephesians 4 in full; the body grows in truth and love, and the chapter subsequently addresses falsehood, harmful speech and generosity.
- **1022, 1 John 3:18**: retains Augustine's encouragement to start by sharing ordinary resources. Adds Benedict's closely related justice distinction: a voluntary gift cannot replace what another person is owed. The unpaid-wages sentence is a concrete editorial application, not a claimed papal quotation. Read 1 John 3 in full, especially 16–18. Existing links on love received and Teresa's ordinary deeds remain distinct: they do not explain the prior obligation of justice.
- **337, 2 Maccabees 12:43**: preserves wrongdoing, resurrection, prayer, Eucharistic intercession and the distinction from purchasing salvation. Adds §48's explanation that interconnected lives and prayer remain meaningful after death. Read 12:38–45, including the illicit amulets and explicit reasoning from resurrection.
- **papal-judgement-and-hope-for-victims**: Revelation 6:9–11 with Romans 12:19–21. Read both full chapters. This is an editorial thematic comparison using Benedict's account of judgement and resurrection; it is not presented as a quotation link or as his explicit comparison of these two passages. It differs from the existing enemy-love link by explaining why final judgement is hope for past victims, whom subsequent historical progress cannot restore. It retains present responsibility and refuses private retaliation.
