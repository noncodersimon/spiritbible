# Benedict XVI: love, Eucharist and the word

## Scope and actual source reading

This focused supplement contains three existing-note enrichments, two new notes and one linked reading. It does not claim an exhaustive survey of Benedict's writings.

Read complete supporting sections in their actual official English texts, cached from canonical Vatican URLs:

- **Deus Caritas Est 5–8 and 12–18.** These provide the context of embodied desire and self-giving love, the need to receive love in order to give it, sacramental communion, and the reciprocal deepening of prayer and neighbourly service.
- **Sacramentum Caritatis 66–71.** Read the relationship of Eucharistic celebration and adoration, the adjacent practical discussion of devotion, and the whole-life worship flowing from Eucharistic transformation. The supplement uses 66 and 70–71, rather than presenting the intervening pastoral instructions as biblical exegesis.
- **Verbum Domini 52–56.** Read the liturgical setting, word and sacrament, the Emmaus/bread-of-life reading, and the carefully analogical discussion of the word's sacramentality. The new card uses the explicit connection in 54–55.
- Reread the actual paragraphs behind the retained Catechism sources: 1611, 2361, 601, 694 and 2100. The final file retains 1611, 694 and 2100; the bodily unity previously supported by 2361 is now directly supported by Benedict's text, and note792/CCC601 remains unchanged outside this supplement.

## Selected changes

- **450, Song of Songs 1:2:** keeps embodied human affection and its spiritual reception; adds Benedict's account of desire maturing into care for the beloved. Avoids importing his Hebrew-word discussion as a simplified lexical rule.
- **881, Romans 12:1:** keeps the living bodily offering and union with Christ's self-offering; deepens the Eucharistic transformation through which daily thoughts, affections and deeds become worship. Retains the distinction between willing love and injury or coercion.
- **818, John 7:38:** keeps the received Spirit becoming a source for others; adds the practical requirement that a giver also receive love and return to Christ. The existing note is a better home than another water card.
- **New, 1 John 4:20:** moves from John's test of professed love to Benedict's insight that serving the neighbour opens our capacity to recognise God's love. Distinct from the existing Trinitarian-love note and Augustine's account of fear becoming desire.
- **New, John 6:56:** introduces Eucharistic adoration as a consequence and deepening of receiving the living Lord, explicitly describing the Church's practice and Benedict's explanation. It does not claim that the Gospel narrates later reservation or adoration practices. Existing notes and links cover sacrifice, life, communion and unity, but not this relation between reception and adoration.
- **New connection, Luke 24:27–32 / John 6:48–51:** Scripture and Eucharist mutually illuminate the same Christ who speaks and gives himself. Benedict explicitly connects these Gospel texts in Verbum Domini 54–55. The existing Emmaus note792 remains unchanged, preserving its distinct insight into hope restored through understanding the cross.

Each new anchor and the full linked verse ranges were read in the actual Bible. Nearby existing notes and relevant connections were compared before writing. Existing IDs, anchors, classifications and established central insights are preserved.

## Deliberate omissions and coordination

A Song8/Luke17/John12 card was considered and omitted: the existing bridal-love card and grain-of-wheat note already carry much of its intended insight. A John7/John19 water card was likewise omitted in favour of enriching818. A revision of792 was drafted but withdrawn after root selected the single linked treatment of Scripture and Eucharist. Eucharistic social unity from Deus Caritas Est14 was not added merely to repeat several existing cards.

Coordinated with the John Paul II editor: their enrichment of900, 1Cor11:26, concerns future resurrection and responsibility for the present world. It does not duplicate this batch's adoration or whole-life worship readings.
