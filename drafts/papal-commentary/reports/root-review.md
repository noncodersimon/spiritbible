# Root author review

Read Fides et Ratio 16–23 in full, following the movement from the Wisdom books through Romans 1 to the wisdom of the Cross. Read Veritatis Splendor 16–24 in full, including the rich young man's relationship to the commandments, discipleship, grace and the Spirit. This is a focused reading of supporting sections, not a review of either entire encyclical.

Selected one enrichment and one new note:

- Note 888, 1 Corinthians 1:23: retain Chrysostom's unearned saving love and the qualification that pain is not good merely as pain. Add John Paul II's account of the Cross challenging self-enclosed rational systems and opening thought to love. The attribution does not portray faith as hostility to reason.
- Matthew 19:21: the existing chapter note concerns marriage and has no overlapping anchor. The new note follows the actual context in verses 16–26: moral enquiry, commandments, the personal invitation, possessions, sorrow and grace. John Paul II's reading joins moral conduct with sharing Christ's life. It does not reduce the demand to give to the poor to an entirely inward feeling.

Considered but did not add a separate faith/reason/creation connection: the existing Wisdom 13 and Romans 1 notes already explain the intellectual value of creation and enquiry. The distinctive new philosophical insight is placed at the Cross rather than repeated through another general card.

Exact evidence snippets are checked against cached primary documents; existing Chrysostom evidence is preserved. Independent review by the prophets editor passed; the retained Chrysostom evidence was extended through the explicit reference to the enemy and offender.
