Reviewed all three revised notes, the revised heavenly-worship connection and all four new connections. No blocking doctrinal, contextual or sourcing issue found.

I read the actual cached pages around the relevant Catechism paragraphs, not only the supplied evidence excerpts:2831–2837;1085–1090;1391–1392;232–233 and 249–260. Relevant contexts for 460,1997,1364–1368 and 1137–1139 were also read during my assigned audit. The actual Bible endpoint ranges and surrounding context were checked; own-scope passages had already been read in full chapters, and the external Matthew,John,Luke,Numbers andDeuteronomy ranges were reread directly.

## Findings by item

- **704, daily bread:** The material, qualitative, Eucharistic and eschatological senses accurately followCCC 2831–2837. The note avoids presenting the spiritual reading as the only lexical meaning and retains the neighbour's bodily hunger. Fundamental is appropriate. Its wording that *epiousios* is rare is preferable to reproducing the source's compressed wording about its New Testament occurrence.
- **993, once-for-all offering:** CCC 1085 explicitly supports the event's participation in divine eternity;CCC 1367–1368 supports one sacrifice and the Church's participation. The note does not imply another crucifixion or an unfinished redemption. The explanation is clear and materially stronger than a bare repeated/not-repeated contrast.
- **1013, participation in divine nature:** CCC 460 and 1997 support adoptive sonship, the Spirit's life and participation through grace. The Creator–creature distinction is explicit, and 2 Peter 1:5–7 grounds the closing moral consequence. Fundamental is appropriate.
- **lamb-and-heavenly-worship:** All five endpoints fit the argument. Christ's wounds remain inJohn 20;Revelation's Lamb and wedding feast andHebrews' heavenly assembly provide the canonical comparison. CCC 1139 and 1085 justify sacramental participation without a second death. The title makes the Catholic interpretive purpose clear without claiming that Revelation is a literal order of Mass.
- **final-bread-and-trinitarian-life:** CCC 1392 precisely supplies the otherwise easily missed step from Baptism to nourishment by the Eucharist. Ephesians 2:18 and 2 Peter 1:4 support the Trinitarian and participatory horizon. The ordinary/spiritual bread relation retains the material meaning. Fundamental is appropriate.
- **final-one-god-father-son-spirit:** The one divine name is explicit inCCC 233, apostolic prayer in 249, full deity/distinction in 253–255, and 1 Corinthians 8:6 language in 258. The account avoids three gods, divided deity or merely three roles. It is a useful canonical synthesis rather than a repeated local Trinity definition.
- **final-zeal-and-self-offering:** Cyril 13.2 explicitly compares Phinehas killing another with Jesus offering himself and quotes 1 Timothy 2:6. The attribution and introductory description of the Numbers episode are accurate. Numbers 25:8 directly confirms the plague ending, with 11–13 giving the theological assessment selected as endpoint. The final ethical application is a responsible Christian reading, not presented as Cyril's verbatim words or as the narrative's initial literal command. Fundamental is defensible because it corrects a consequential misuse of violent zeal.
- **final-martha-mary-love-in-deeds:** TeresaVII.4.17 explicitly joins Martha andMary;21–23 explicitly turns grand plans towards service of nearby companions and values deeds by their love. Luke 10,John 15 and 1 John 3 fit the declared spiritual application. Teresa's older conflation of Mary figures and her passage's anti-Jewish language are not imported into the commentary. Interest correctly marks this attributed application. It overlaps locally with Augustine's 1 John 3:18 advice to begin with attainable generosity, but its prayer–service integration and Martha/Mary frame are distinct enough to retain.
