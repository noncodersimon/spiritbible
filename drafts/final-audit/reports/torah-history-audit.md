## Principal additions

- Genesis: distinguish domination from created human equality (3:16); explain divine sorrow through Augustine without making God surprised (6:6); reject racial misuse of Canaan's curse (9:25); distinguish patriarchal polygamy from marriage's fullness (29:30).
- Leviticus: distinguish ritual uncleanness from moral blame (12:2); affirm personal dignity and reject violence at the severe sexual-law penalty (20:13), using the current CCC 2267.
- Numbers: acknowledge the interpretive severity of Moses' command to kill children and captive women (31:17), without inventing a historical excuse or deriving permission for Christian violence.
- Deuteronomy: explain free faith and religious liberty at the execution command (13:9); distinguish assault from willing participation and reject silence as proof of consent (22:26).
- Canonical links: Lamech's vengeance and unlimited forgiveness; Enoch in Genesis/Hebrews; covenant rite and God's later oath; slavery regulation and personal dignity; earthly sanctuary and heavenly pattern; prolonged bleeding and Christ's healing; firstfruits and bodily resurrection; Aaron's intercession as explicitly reread in Wisdom; heart conversion as both command and gift; eunuch exclusion, Isaiah's promise and baptism in Acts; the ox law quoted for material support of ministers.

The Genesis 15 covenant link explicitly distinguishes its rite from the later Genesis 22 oath discussed in Hebrews 6. The Enoch link does not invent a detailed account of his destination. The Lamech link does not claim that this translation uses identical numerical formulas in Genesis and Matthew. The eunuch link explicitly distinguishes Acts' quotation of Isaiah 53 from our canonical comparison with Isaiah 56.

## Important refinements

- Genesis 9:6 now cites the current Catechism death-penalty teaching, alongside human dignity, rather than leaving the ancient sanction open to modern misapplication.
- Jacob's ladder connection uses Augustine's actual movement between contemplation and accessible teaching, preserving a distinct insight beyond the local Christological note.
- Exodus memorial language retains the Catechism's stronger account of saving events becoming present, without flattening Eucharistic memorial into recollection.
- Exodus 19 priesthood and 20:7 misuse of God's name have source locators expanded to the paragraphs actually supporting the Christian applications.
- The existing finger/Spirit connection now includes Exodus 8:19 alongside the tablets and Gospel comparison.
- Jubilee notes and the existing release connection acknowledge that the same legislation permits permanent foreign slavery. Neither the Old Testament nor New Testament household regulations are falsely labelled comprehensive abolition laws.
- The thanksgiving-offering note no longer invents a reason for the meal's time limit. Levitical disease language is distinguished from a precise modern diagnosis without adopting dated medical claims from the Encyclopedia.
- The restoration connection adds 2 Corinthians 2:6–8: discipline can become sufficient, and love must be reaffirmed before sorrow overwhelms. The warning against using private correction to prohibit reporting crime remains.
- Adjacent Moses notes in Numbers distinguish intercession for Miriam from contemplation in service. Deuteronomy retains the YHWH/Adonai/Kyrios explanation and combines its duplicated display citation.
- The testimony rule is not offered as a reason to dismiss abuse reports without a second eyewitness. The roof-railing note retains practical prevention without demanding impossible foresight.
