## Additions and connection changes

- **2 Kings 13:21 — “Holiness includes the body”** (`FINAL-HISTORY-LATE-001`, fundamental). The exact anchor “touched Elisha’s bones” is unique and does not overlap an existing note. Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechetical Lecture 18.16, explicitly reads Elisha's bones and Paul's cloths as signs that bodily holiness participates in God's work. The note identifies this as Cyril's patristic interpretation and keeps God as giver of life.
- **Tobit 12:12–15 / Revelation 8:3–4** (`final-angels-present-prayer`, fundamental). Raphael's presentation of prayer and Revelation's angel with incense place earthly petition before God. CCC 335–336 and the Catholic Encyclopedia article “Raphael” support angelic praise and intercession. Coordinated with root so it complements the separate Revelation 5 liturgy synthesis.
- **1 Maccabees 4:56–59 / 2 Maccabees 10:6–8 / John 10:22–23** (`final-dedication-and-jesus`, interest). Gives the Gospel's Feast of Dedication its Maccabean historical setting. Sources distinguish the festival's origin from the Catechism's account of Jesus' reverence for the Temple.
- **Faith and medicine**: added Wisdom 16:10–12 to `deep-faith-and-medicine`, preserving all original endpoints. Coordination with the wisdom reviewer confirmed the full chapter's serpent context. “Neither herb nor poultice” concerns that particular deliverance and does not overturn Sirach's approval of medical care. The summary explicitly supplies the context to avoid a dangerous generalisation.

## Principal cuts and corrections

Repeated applications, generic exhortations, multiple formulations of one insight and defensive closing paragraphs were removed. Most notes now move from the concrete passage to one sourced interpretation and a proportionate implication. Secondary spiritual applications remain “interest”; foundational doctrines and consequential corrections are “fundamental”. These priorities grade editorial necessity, not truth.

Significant accuracy decisions include:

- Elijah's care includes food, rest and bodily vulnerability; the widow's promise is particular, not a general test requiring people to endanger themselves. Tobit's blindness is not evidence that righteous conduct has failed.
- Elisha's feeding account retains the Bible's twenty loaves and a hundred people. Ambrose's differing number is not silently imported into Scripture.
- Nehemiah 9's application now uses CCC 2586; CCC 2585's explicit Nehemiah reference is to chapter 1. Nehemiah 8:10 connects joy with providing food for those without it, supported by an exact full CCC 2447 excerpt.
- Ezra's historical household crisis is distinguished from Christian teaching on mixed marriages using CCC 1633–1637. The related connection preserves Paul's instruction concerning a willing unbelieving spouse.
- Clement's praise of Judith's courage is not treated as endorsement of every means. CCC 1789 supplies the necessary moral distinction. Judith's violence is not converted into a general permission.
- Greek Esther is treated according to the actual supplied text; differences concerning plunder are not erased by importing a standard summary of Hebrew Esther. The Greek prayers and Catholic canonical reception remain explicit.
- Resurrection in 2 Maccabees includes bodily life; prayer for the dead and Jeremiah's intercession remain distinguished from merely commemorating departed heroes.
- Razis's death is not presented as a moral pattern. CCC 2282–2283 retain diminished responsibility and hope rather than assigning an individual's final fate.
- Historical connections are presented as comparisons or canonical readings where appropriate. No unsupported direct literary dependence, speculative etymology or fabricated historical harmonisation was added.
