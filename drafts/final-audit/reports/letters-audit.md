## What changed

The principal work is concision with substance preserved. Existing note prose has a median of 122 words; revised prose has a median of 70(words counted without bottom references). Revised notes range 63–84 words. Each was rewritten after reading its argument and local context; this was not automatic sentence deletion. Repeated framing, multiple examples of the same point and speculative warnings were removed. Necessary distinctions remain where losing them would change the meaning.

Examples of substantive refinement:

- Romans 4:4 now citesCCC 2007–2008 for grace-enabled merit, rather than making the existing 2007 reference support the whole account by itself.
- 1 Corinthians 3:13 names the Catholic understanding of Purgatory while distinguishing Paul's immediate building-work imagery from the later doctrinal application.
- 1 Corinthians 10:13 is explicitly about temptation, with 2 Corinthians 1:8 showing why it cannot become a guarantee that trauma never exceeds human strength.
- Philippians 4:13 retains the decisive hunger/plenty context; Colossians 1:15 preserves firstborn's pre-eminence without treating the Son as a creature.
- 2 Corinthians 5:20 distinguishes apostolic reconciliation ministry from the whole Church's witness.2 Corinthians 5:21 receives a separate concise clarification about the sinless Christ's solidarity, coordinated with the Gospels' cry-of-abandonment treatment.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:23 preserves the Catechism's explicit explanation of spirit/soul/body without suggesting three separate human substances.
- 1 Timothy 4:8 and the two Cloud notes are shortened after rereading their actual primary chapters. They retain their specific insights: practices serve love; consolation is no ranking of holiness; contemplation does not claim to master God conceptually.
- Hebrews 6:6 retains Chrysostom's specific distinction between unrepeatable baptismal renewal and the continuing possibility of repentance. The interpretation remains attributed rather than represented as an uncontested literal solution.
- 1 Peter 2:21 retains an explicit safeguard against requiring someone to remain in abuse;1 Peter 4:8 clarifies that covering sins cannot mean concealing harm. Both now foreground the positive theological claim more economically.
- Revelation's symbolic readings retain historical and interpretive distinctions. The 666 proposal remains attributed historical scholarship; Marian and ecclesial readings of the woman retain separate evidence; the new name remains anchored to 2:17, not Christ's own new name in 3:12.

## Selected additions

Eleven new local notes are supplied, ten fundamental and one interest:

1. Romans 3:8: a good intended outcome cannot make an evil means good(CCC 1755–1756).
2. Romans 9:21: Chrysostom warns against extending the potter analogy into a denial of free will(Homily 16).
3. Romans 16:7: Chrysostom's recognition of Junia as a woman honoured among apostles, without claiming a settled account of her precise office(Homily 31;interest).
4. 1 Corinthians 7:4: the reciprocal bodily gift of marriage never establishes a right to coercion(CCC 1645,2356).
5. 1 Corinthians 15:14: Christian hope depends on the resurrection as God's act, not merely an inspiring memory(CCC 651).
6. 2 Corinthians 5:21: being made sin does not mean Christ personally sinned or was rejected as a sinner(CCC 602–603).
7. Galatians 5:17: flesh/Spirit opposition concerns moral resistance to grace, not contempt for the body(CCC 2515–2516).
8. 1 Timothy 2:15: childbirth cannot be an independent means of salvation or a universal condition excluding childless women; the note states a doctrinal boundary without claiming to resolve every disputed exegetical detail(CCC 1618–1620,2010).
9. James 2:10: the commandments' organic unity does not make all sins equally grave(CCC 2069,1854).
10. 1 John 3:9: new birth is incompatible with sin but does not make the baptised incapable of falling; read alongside 1:8–2:1 andCCC 1425–1426.
11. Revelation 20:5: Augustine's present spiritual resurrection and symbolic millennium, explicitly retaining future bodily resurrection;CCC 676 supplies a compact boundary against earthly messianic political fulfilment(City of God 20.7–9).

Three new connections are supplied:

- Exodus 16:16–18 /2 Corinthians 8:12–15: Paul's explicit manna quotation gives material sharing its pattern of mutual sufficiency; actual Chrysostom 2 CorHomily 17.2 checked. Torah owns the existing Exodus local note and approved this division.
- 2 Corinthians 6:14–16 /1 Corinthians 5:9–10: rejection of idolatrous loyalty does not require withdrawal from non-Christian neighbours;CCC 1932–1933. The original mixed-marriage endpoint was removed after finding the existing Ezra/mixed-households link, preventing duplication.
- 1 Thessalonians 2:14–16 /Revelation 2:9 /Revelation 3:9 /Romans 11:28–29: fierce polemical language cannot establish collective or inherited Jewish guilt or contempt. The comparison retains the Jewish Christian exemplars within Thessalonians and Israel's irrevocable calling in Romans;CCC 597 and 839. This replaces a proposed local 1 Thessalonians note so that the point appears once as a meaningful canonical comparison.

## Coordination and rejected additions

No quota was used for new material. Many passages remain without local notes because nearby explanations or related passages already carry the significant point.

- Enoch/Hebrews 11, Abraham's oath/Hebrews 6, Levitical firstfruits/1 Corinthians 15 and Exodus 21/Philemon/Ephesians 6 slavery-and-dignity connections are owned by Torah. No competing additions were made.
- Wisdom owns Wisdom 7:26/Hebrews 1:3 radiance; Gospels owns the keys connection's possibleRevelation 3:7 endpoint and Matthew 6:13's prayer-wording clarification.
- The restoration connection begins at Leviticus 19:17, so its ownership was clarified with Torah and Gospels before the agreed 2 Corinthians 2:6–8 endpoint extension. The letters group does not duplicate that change.
- Prophets owns Ezekiel 9/Revelation 7 sealing and possibleJoel/Revelation 14 harvest andEzekiel 38/Revelation 20 Gog echoes. Ezekiel's eaten scroll/Revelation 10 was suggested to that agent only if actual source support warrants it.
- Root owns Hebrews' single sacrifice, heavenly liturgy/Eucharist,1 Corinthians 8:6 and broad participation-in-divine-life synthesis.
- No speculative identification of the restrainer(2 Thessalonians 2), horsemen, modern technologies, present nations or apocalyptic dates was added.
- No historical rationale was invented for 1 Corinthians 14:34–35 or 1 Timothy 2:12. These remain difficult passages deserving fuller specialist treatment than an unsupported quick note.
- Additional general observations on faith/works, unity, gifts, generosity, marital love, suffering and contemplative experience were declined where existing local notes or links already supply them.
- Hebrews 12:5–11 discipline and Revelation 14:4 virginity were considered. Their full contextual interpretation would need a focused source treatment; no thin caveat was added merely to fill space. Existing notes address providence, suffering, bodily dignity and complementary vocations elsewhere.
