# Gospels and Acts — final refinement audit

## Scope and actual reading

All 117 chapters of Matthew, Mark, Luke, John and Acts were read in their actual Bible-baseline text, in manageable batches. Every one of the 205 existing notes was read in full beside its passage; this was not a title or keyword audit. All 85 existing connections touching these books were read in full for comparison. ## Completed decisions

- 205 existing notes: 204 revised; daily-bread note 704 retained for root's separately documented synthesis. No existing IDs or anchors changed.
- Existing note priorities: 160 fundamental, 45 interest. These describe explanatory role, not truth or quality.
- All 24 owned connections revised; 23 fundamental and the Teresa/Christ-humanity application classified interest. Existing IDs and endpoints preserved.
- 8 new notes and 4 new connections. The Jesus Prayer and Augustine's image/coin application are interest connections; the two direct scriptural quotation connections are fundamental.
- No introductions changed and no production files edited.

Existing-note prose falls from approximately 25,489 to 13,770 words (46.0% shorter), excluding source-link labels. Revised notes are naturally brief explanations, presently 57–79 words each. This reflects individually rewritten explanations rather than a sentence-deletion process. There was no minimum-word target beyond the meaningful schema floor.

## Material improvements

The revisions remove repeated assurances, stock endings and multiple restatements of the same point. Necessary distinctions remain where they protect the actual meaning: real human and divine knowledge in Christ; mercy and protection; forgiveness and memory; illness and possession; grace and human cooperation; spiritual interpretation and literal event; Eucharist and the bodily reality of the Incarnation.

Examples of substantive clarification:

- Matthew 5:3 now frames the Beatitudes through the human desire for happiness (CCC1718), then explains poverty of spirit through voluntary humility (CCC2546). Matthew5:8 specifies charity, chastity and truthful faith rather than treating purity vaguely.
- Mark3:14 explains the Twelve against Israel's tribes and ecclesial purpose. Mark8:29 places messianic secrecy beside mistaken political expectations and the Passion. Abba is explained soberly as Father without the unsupported sentimental gloss Daddy.
- John8:12 uses the actual short John Paul II quotation, “The whole of Christ’s life was a continual teaching,” explicitly as quoted in CCC561. No new papal document or invented quotation was introduced.
- John6:44 retains Augustine's attraction through desire and delight, distinct from the prophets group's non-coercion note at Hosea11. John16:13 focuses complete revelation and deepening understanding, avoiding repetition of private-revelation discernment.
- Acts18:26 retains the specific witness of Priscilla and Aquila as married lay catechists rather than repeating generic teachability. Acts20:28 carefully preserves the person/nature distinction in language about God's blood.
- Teresa's Matthew6:6 note distinguishes attentive vocal/mental prayer from technique. The John14:23 note is reduced to indwelling and active service; the Christ-humanity link preserves her warning against abandoning the Gospel in advanced prayer.
- The five Eucharist, Baptism, vine/body and mission connections now state their positive theological connection directly. They retain shared divine life, ecclesial unity and responsibility without a succession of speculative objections.
- Peter's keys/shepherding connection adds Isaiah22:20–22 and Revelation3:7, preserving all three former endpoints. Isaiah supplies the entrusted-steward imagery; Revelation applies its opening/shutting language to Christ. Peter's authority remains derivative and ordered to feeding the flock.

## Selected additions and why they matter

1. Matthew27:25: the Catechism's explicit rejection of inherited or collective Jewish guilt, including the Christian reader's own responsibility for sin. This corrects a consequential misuse of the passage.
2. Matthew17:15: illness must not be diagnosed as possession from a narrative resemblance; CCC1673 expressly distinguishes medical illness and demonic affliction.
3. Matthew13:55: the Catholic reading of Jesus' brothers as close relations, with the Catechism's identification of another Mary. No unsupported Greek etymology was added.
4. Matthew5:34: truthful speech and legitimate solemn oaths for grave and just reasons, as CCC2154 directly interprets Jesus' command.
5. Luke11:28: Mary herself exemplifies the blessedness of receiving the word in faith; CCC506 quotes Augustine on faith's deeper blessedness.
6. Mark16:18: signs of protection do not command manufactured danger; CCC2119 distinguishes trust from testing God. Paul's accidental snakebite supplies the biblical comparison within the note.
7. Matthew6:13: the petition asks for strength against entering/yielding to temptation; it does not make God the author of enticement to evil. The distinction between temptation and consent is retained briefly.
8. Acts5:4: Chrysostom's explicit distinction between freely retained property and a professed holy gift corrupted by deception. The severe judgement remains visible; the passage is not reduced to compulsory communal ownership.
9. Jesus Prayer connection, Mark10/Luke18: the Catechism directly traces the prayer to the Gospel petitions, offering a concrete spiritual practice grounded in the text.
10. Greatest commandments connection, Mark12/Deuteronomy6/Leviticus19: Jesus' answer joins Israel's actual commands and gives worship and neighbour-love one direction. This is distinct from root's confession of the one God/Trinitarian connection.
11. Caesar's image/God's image, Matthew22/Genesis1: explicitly attributed to Augustine's theological application rather than invented additional Gospel speech.
12. Temple quotation connection, Mark11/Isaiah56/Jeremiah7: prayer for the nations and exposure of religious security covering injustice. The den's significance follows Jeremiah's actual context; the Catechism corroborates Jesus' zeal for the Father's house. It is not claimed that CCC584 supplies a lexical explanation of den.

- No claim is made to have exhausted all possible commentary in these rich books. Distinctness and verified support governed selection.

## Catechism theme check

Fundamental themes already covered in these books include Incarnation, Christ's identity and human life, kingdom, grace and adoption, Beatitudes, mercy and justice, prayer, sacraments, apostolic ministry, moral discernment, resurrection and mission. This pass closes concrete gaps in temptation, solemn oaths, illness/exorcism distinction, Marian family interpretation, communal truthfulness and collective-guilt misreading. Daily/supersubstantial bread and the larger Eucharist/Trinitarian synthesis remain intentionally assigned to root, rather than omitted by accident.

Further useful enquiries, below the priority of verified additions, are a tightly sourced Luke16:8 account of the steward and an explicit divine-bridegroom interpretation at Matthew9:15 if it can add something beyond existing marriage/Cana/Song links. Neither is asserted as a new annotation without checking a sufficiently precise source.

## Verification and limitations

- Production draft validator passes all212 revised/new notes against actual source-cache content, with the authorised25-word floor.
- Exactly205 owned note IDs and24 owned connection IDs are present, once each.
- New anchors are exact, unique and non-overlapping with baseline annotations. Existing anchors are unchanged.
- Every revised/new connection source has exact excerpt evidence and a raw-HTML SHA256 matching its cached page. All endpoint verses exist; Peter's extra endpoints preserve all originals. Connection summaries satisfy35–200words.
- Sources were read in their actual contexts before rewriting. Automatic excerpt/hash checks complement that reading; they do not stand in for it. The only newly fetched page in this latter part was Chrysostom Acts12, read in full. All other source pages were already cached and their relevant contexts were read directly.
- Historical sources' polemical rhetoric is not reproduced as editorial judgement. Augustine's Caesar-image observation is separated from his surrounding anti-Jewish rhetoric; Chrysostom's Acts5 note states his property/deception distinction without importing his entire rhetoric of punishment.
- This is an audited selection from the authorised source collections, not a survey of all Christian literature or a critical apparatus for every textual question. Independent cross-review and integration remain root's responsibility.
