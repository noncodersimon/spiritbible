## Editorial decisions and gaps


### Coverage and concision

- Existing notes: 200/200, each individually compared with its complete original prose and actual Bible context; 136 fundamental, 64 interest. Existing IDs and anchors preserved.
- Existing owned connections: 24/24, including actual endpoint text outside the Prophets and the complete relevant source contexts; all classified fundamental because they express substantial canonical relationships or consequential doctrinal distinctions.
- Other-owned connections touching the assigned books were read for comparison: jubilee-and-release, god-seeks-sheep, and clean-heart-water-spirit.
- Existing note prose reduced from 25,824 to 14,549 words before citations, about 44%. Revised notes run 64–82 words. Existing connection summaries total 1,754 words. Revisions retain each passage's substantive argument rather than cutting to a fixed sentence template.
- All 258 chapters include the complete Catholic Daniel additions, Baruch 6, and Malachi 4; no chapter was skipped because it lacked an existing note.

### Significant additions

Four notes address consequential reading gaps:

1. Isaiah 14:12: the morning-star image first addresses Babylon's ruler; the Latin name Lucifer has positive as well as later fallen-angel usage. Lexical statement carefully distinguishes usage from etymology.
2. Jeremiah 29:11: the promised future belongs to a community facing prolonged exile, not a guarantee of immediate individual prosperity.
3. Ezekiel 20:25: the difficult statutes language cannot sanctify child sacrifice condemned within the same chapter. Irenaeus's interpretation is explicitly attributed and its limits acknowledged; CCC 311 guards against making God the author of moral evil.
4. Amos 1:13: accountability for atrocities crosses covenant and national boundaries; territorial gain cannot erase victims' dignity.

Nine connections add specific relationships not adequately covered by a generic thematic note:

- Isaiah's hardening commission with Mark's difficult parable explanation and Matthew's account of self-closed eyes; Chrysostom's reading remains explicitly attributed.
- The servant's light for nations, Simeon's recognition, and the apostolic mission in Acts 13.
- Ezekiel's forehead mark and Revelation's sealed servants, with Tertullian's Christian cross reception.
- Ezekiel's Gog/Magog and Revelation's worldwide final conflict, with Augustine's rejection of a single enemy ethnicity.
- Ezekiel's eaten scroll and Revelation's sweet/bitter little book: the word is received before it is announced.
- Daniel's desecrated sanctuary and Jesus' warning to flee: faithful endurance and protection of life rather than an invented timetable.
- Joel's ripe harvest and Revelation's judgement imagery.
- Amos's restored Davidic tent and James's recognition of Gentile admission in Acts 15.
- Zechariah's thirty silver pieces and Matthew's betrayal account. The summary explicitly acknowledges Matthew's naming of Jeremiah and does not invent an attribution harmonisation.

### Important corrections and editorial distinctions

The tighter prose preserves necessary distinctions: Israel's original setting versus Christian reception; historical restoration versus bodily resurrection; inherited damage versus personal guilt; moral evil versus God's permission; prophecy versus an individual's interpretation; disability and dignity versus a supposed healing test.

Specific improvements include Daniel 3's own identification of the deliverer as God's angel rather than forcing the king's phrase into a precise Christological identification; Hosea's parental mercy read through the following 'God, and not man'; Ezekiel's exiles as God's people despite Jerusalem's exclusionary claim; honest reporting and protection of those coerced; and Malachi's Eucharistic reception without claiming that the generic word minhah alone establishes the Eucharist. The note on Isaiah 35 now cites CCC 547–549 directly for Jesus' embodied messianic signs.

Independent reviewer deep_letters read all existing decisions, endpoints and actual supporting contexts. Accepted refinements expanded narrow evidence snippets for CCC 1044, 239, 953, 536, Augustine's Daniel/David interpretation, Malachi's Eucharistic reception and Lucifer's positive usage; corrected 'chapter36'; and clarified Matthew's Jeremiah attribution. Review of all four additions found no substantive doctrinal problem. Final review report is separate.
