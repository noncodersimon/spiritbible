## Significant additions

Eight new notes, all fundamental:

- Job38:26: rain in uninhabited wilderness discloses creation's own goodness beyond immediate human utility (CCC339).
- Psalm51:4: sin against God does not erase harm to neighbours; the Catechism joins the verse to the injury sin does to human solidarity (CCC1849–1850).
- Proverbs9:5: distinguish the literal banquet of wisdom from Augustine's explicit Incarnation/Eucharist reception (City of God XVII.20).
- Proverbs23:13: parental formation serves the child's dignity; ancient rod language is not a medical guarantee or unlimited permission to harm (CCC2222–2223). One focused clarification, not repeated warnings on every disciplinary proverb.
- Ecclesiastes9:5: death viewed from life under the sun is not the full Christian account of the afterlife; resurrection hope unfolds progressively (CCC992–994).
- Sirach12:4: prudent concern about enabling harm is distinguished from making moral deservingness the condition of Christian neighbour-love (CCC122,1933).
- Sirach25:24: the fall involves both first parents and does not establish women's moral inferiority (CCC404–405,369).
- Sirach33:27: identify the ancient slaveholding setting while clearly stating the Church's condemnation of enslavement and torture (CCC2414,2297).

One new fundamental connection: Wisdom7:25–26/Hebrews1:2–3, the personified wisdom's light and the Son's radiance. CE explicitly compares the passages; CCC241–242 supports the divine Christological reception. Coordinated with the letters agent: it adds the canonical route from Wisdom rather than another free-standing account of the Son's divinity.

## Main refinements and necessary distinctions

The existing notes generally carried sound insights but often repeated the same pastoral caution or added several generic applications. Revisions make the source's distinctive insight central: truth in intention at Psalm15, baptismal longing at Psalm42, bodily rest versus forgetting God at Psalm63, enduring gifts through flawed ministers at Psalm127, and speech/prudence across Proverbs/Sirach. The source remains identified as an individual Father's reading where appropriate.

Preserved consequential qualifications: Job's anguish does not prove personal guilt; spiritual readings do not erase literal human love in the Song; original contexts remain real when passages are received Christologically; bodily existence is good; grief and pain are not holiness tests; medical treatment and prayer belong together; forgiveness is not approval of injury. Psalm137 still names the literal violence before explaining Augustine's spiritual redirection against nascent destructive desires. Psalm88 retains its unresolved darkness. Psalm22 retains both real anguish and the Son's unbroken redeeming love with the Father.

Corrected an overprecise authoring-draft assertion: CCC303 cites Proverbs19:21, not16:9; the revised16:9 note says it develops the same insight. Removed a duplicated CE link in the Sirach1:1 note. Did not turn the Psalm40/Hebrews10 wording difference into an unsupported Hebrew/Greek explanation: its evidence supports the Incarnation and the shared movement into an offered life, not the full textual history.

Song notes451/456 retain separate functions: seeking the beloved in contemplation versus receiving encounter into ordinary life. No stable note IDs or existing connections were deleted. Existing cross-book links were shortened while retaining their actual textual relation (quotation, echo, theme or reception).
