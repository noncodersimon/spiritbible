# Joshua, Judges and Ruth: final editorial audit

## Reading completed

All 49 chapters were read as actual verse text, alongside every existing note in full. Reading batches were kept small enough to avoid truncated chapter output:

- Joshua 1–5, 6–10, 11–16, 17–21 and 22–24: all 24 chapters.
- Judges 1–5, 6–9, 10–14, 15–18 and 19–21: all 21 chapters.
- Ruth 1–4: all four chapters.

Every sentence of the 35 baseline notes was considered against that context. Both existing connections touching these books were read; these are also the two owned connections because their first endpoints are Joshua and Ruth. Mark 6:17–29 was separately read for the new connection. The source contexts listed below were actually consulted, not inferred from citation metadata.

## Decisions and results

All 35 existing notes (IDs 151–185) receive an individual revision and significance classification: 30 fundamental and five interest. The secondary insights are Joshua 4:6 and 22:27; Judges 5:2 and 6:15; Ruth 4:10. Both owned links are revised and fundamental. Existing note IDs, anchor phrases and link endpoints remain intact.

The existing main note prose falls from approximately 4,379 to 2,328 whitespace-counted words, excluding bottom citation paragraphs. Each revision retains a particular interpretive or practical point. These are individually written revisions, not truncations of source paragraphs. The revisions currently range from 62 to 72 words.

One new fundamental note explains Micah's presumption in Judges 17:13: possessing a priest does not guarantee divine favour while his household practises idolatry. CCC 2111 supplies the precise distinction between religious practice and magical reliance on its external performance. This addresses a meaningful gap within an otherwise unannotated episode.

One new fundamental connection pairs Judges 11:30–35 with Mark 6:22–28. Ambrose himself pairs Jephthah and Herod in Duties 1.50.264, explaining why an oath cannot make harming an innocent person a duty. The existing Jephthah note retains the local tragedy and the distinction between the Spirit's gift and every subsequent action. The link makes the explicit patristic cross-book comparison available at both passages. The Gospels reviewer confirmed there is no competing Herod/Jephthah addition.

## Principal corrections and cuts

Joshua's typological notes distinguish the literal homecoming from later Christian reception. The conquest note states Catholic condemnation of extermination without claiming to dissolve the ancient narrative's historical difficulty. The Achan note distinguishes shared consequences from equal personal guilt or permission to punish relatives. The oath note preserves the exception for a promise to do evil. Prosperity and military success are not offered as universal guarantees of religious observance.

Judges' repetition is explained as a literary and theological framework. The revised Gideon notes distinguish patient reassurance from demanding private signs, grace from poor preparation, and good professions from accountable conduct. Samson's consecration and ability are distinguished from maturity; Ambrose's actual argument about courage needing justice sharpens the point. The abused woman in Judges 19 remains visible as a person rather than a rhetorical device for male honour. The final refrain is connected with the coerced marriages immediately preceding it, while kingship itself remains accountable to moral truth.

Ruth's existing coverage is already substantial for four chapters. Revisions foreground her agency, access to safe work, practical stewardship, and public responsibility. The genealogy reveals a wider reach without making a famous descendant the measure of Ruth's worth. Grief is neither corrected prematurely nor made unreal by later hope.

- Justin Martyr, Dialogue 111–113: Rahab's scarlet-thread reading and Joshua's temporary inheritance compared with Christ's eternal gift. The notes do not adopt surrounding polemics or every historical assertion in this older translation.
- Ambrose, Duties 1.35.175–180; 1.40.205 onward; 1.50.264–268; and 2.26.131–135: justice and fortitude, Gideon, harmful oaths, and Samson. The Samson citation locator now explicitly includes paragraph 176, which supplies the justice principle.

## Candidates declined and thematic gap assessment

- Further Rahab/scarlet-cord, Jordan/baptism and Joshua/rest material would repeat established coverage. The two existing connections are tightened instead.
- Cities of refuge and inheritance rights are significant, but these books reprise legislation whose primary explanation belongs with the Torah audit; no second general-purpose note is added merely to increase coverage.
- Shibboleth and the garment/wings language in Ruth could support lexical commentary, but no newly verified lexical source was available in the permitted corpus. No Hebrew explanation is inferred from memory.
- A separate Judges 21 forced-marriage note would duplicate the newly sharpened final-refrain note's treatment of coercion and protection. Root may choose a future direct consent link if the wider collection requires it.
- High-priority Catechism themes here are moral limits in war (Joshua 5, Judges 19–21), truthful commitments bounded by justice (Joshua 9, Judges 11), authentic worship versus superstition (Judges 17), and provision for vulnerable people (Ruth 2–4). The revised set addresses each. No additional major sacramental synthesis is claimed; root owns that cross-Bible work.
