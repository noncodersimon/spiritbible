## Findings

No material accuracy, unsupported doctrinal claim or significant loss of substance requires a rewrite before integration. The shortening generally keeps one identifiable insight, its reason, and the qualifications needed for the passage. It removes repeated general cautions and application sentences without simply turning the notes into summaries of plot.

The 77 fundamental / 50 interest classifications are coherent. Fundamental notes tend to unlock covenant, worship, bodily resurrection, authority, moral limits or a consequential misunderstanding. Interest notes enrich particular scenes through habits of attention, service, companionship, craft or memory. The new relic-related note reasonably counts as fundamental because it explains a distinctive Christian reception of an otherwise puzzling bodily miracle. The historical Dedication connection reasonably counts as interest; the angelic-prayer connection bears more doctrinal weight.

The revised Ezra marriage material correctly distinguishes the ancient expulsion from Paul's instruction and Catholic teaching about mixed marriages. The bodily-resurrection notes preserve the whole person and the difference between a return to ordinary life and final resurrection. Razis's death is not made a model of holiness, and the added reference to diminished responsibility under anguish or torture improves the pastoral accuracy. Medicine and faith remain compatible, with Wisdom 16 carefully confined to the serpent episode. The captivity/Samaritan link explicitly declines to prove literary dependence from thematic similarities.

The new Elisha-bones note is faithful to Cyril's actual reasoning in Lecture 18.16, including his comparison with Paul's cloths. It presents the interpretation as patristic reception and keeps God as the giver of life. The new Tobit/Revelation connection uses genuine prayer imagery at both endpoints. The Dedication link is an accurate historical identification, not an invented typology.

No revision is requested merely for personal style. A few recurring forms (“The Catechism...”, “The passage...”) reflect the collection's citation and explanatory method; they do not obstruct understanding enough to justify another rewriting pass.

## Nonblocking evidence-record improvements

Two excerpts are accurate but too short to demonstrate the most particular claim in the note. The cited pages themselves directly support the prose, so this is internal provenance improvement rather than a correction to the reader-facing content:

- **Note 234, 1 Kings 19:12:** the recorded excerpt is “Elijah, like Moses before him, hides”. The full CCC 2583 paragraph explicitly proceeds to Moses and Elijah beholding the unveiled face of Christ at the Transfiguration. Including that sentence would better support the note's “fulfilled in Christ” claim.
- **Note 277, Nehemiah 2:4:** the excerpt ends after learning prayer at certain moments. CCC 2659 continues, “but his Spirit is offered us at all times, in the events of each day, to make prayer spring up from us.” That is the more direct evidence for the note's everyday-circumstances claim.

All revised/new note excerpt strings were also checked against normalized cached page text; no missing excerpt was found.
