# Contemplative source review

Read thirteen primary chapters from Teresa, Catherine and the anonymous Cloud, after verifying actual editions and catalogue URLs. Selected five distinct local notes and one connection. These are explicitly later spiritual applications, except Teresa’s direct reception of John14:23 and John14:6/9. Each maintains the biblical context and distinguishes a saint’s experience from a condition imposed on all Christians.

- Matthew6:6: prayer as attentive relationship; no existing annotation here.
- John14:23: Trinity’s indwelling expressed in active service; no current note.
- 1Corinthians13:12: contemplative love when thought reaches limits, expressly retaining study and reflection.
- 2Corinthians5:7: present faith/future sight distinguished from later application to differing consolations. No ranking of holiness by feelings.
- 1Timothy4:8: disciplines serve charity, with legitimate adaptation, rather than competition in severity.
- John14:6–9/1John1:1–3: Teresa’s specific correction of spiritual practice that outgrows Christ’s humanity, distinct from current general incarnation notes.

## Independent review revisions

Wisdom reviewer checked the primary chapters and current context. Omitted Catherine at1Cor12:21: despite good provenance, its central insight duplicated the adjacent12:22 note and vine/body connection. Cloud50 note now centres on refusing to rank holiness by consolations, distinguishing it from the existing Song3:1 dryness note. Teresa atJohn14:23 says extraordinary experiences, avoiding an unnecessary visual implication.

## Candidates omitted

Teresa VII.4 on Martha/Mary duplicates the existing Augustine note at Luke10:42. Cloud34 on grace duplicates the existing John15:5 note. Cloud24/Catherine on neighbour love largely repeat existing charity notes. Psalm131’s weaned child might host a consolations application, but 2Corinthians5:7 gives the clearer distinct placement; do not repeat it. Song5 was considered but no passage-specific insight from these thirteen source chapters justified attaching a general mystical saying. Teresa I.2 self-knowledge/humility was not used merely to broaden author coverage.

## Editions and limitations

CCEL Interior Castle: Stanbrook Benedictines translation revised by Benedict Zimmerman, third edition1921. Dialogue: Algar Thorold translation. Cloud: anonymous medieval work, Evelyn Underhill edition1922; Underhill is not presented as the author. Public-domain-era texts are paraphrased in original accessible prose; internal short evidence excerpts are retained for review. The distinct voices and claims of spiritual classics are not labelled universal doctrine.
