# Gospel and Acts deeper review

Draft: `gospels.json`, 25 new notes and five new connections. No production files changed. This is selective expansion, not an attempt to annotate every uncovered chapter.

## Review method

Reviewed the existing notes in all five assigned books (Matthew 40, Mark 27, Luke 40, John 47, Acts 25), together with the summaries of every existing connection touching these books. Compared the proposed anchors with the actual current Bible text and the nearest existing explanations. Read the cited Catechism sections and six patristic passages in the cached source pages. Source excerpts in the JSON are internal verification material; the prose is original and the displayed references are abbreviated links.

The current coverage is already strong on the major Christological claims, Mary, baptism, Eucharist, the Passion, discipleship, forgiveness, prayer and standard Old Testament fulfilments. Expansion therefore concentrates on particular interpretive difficulties, overlooked narrative details, several useful patristic applications and the practical theology of Acts.

## Book-by-book decisions

### Matthew — four notes

- 5:5: Chrysostom's explanation of meekness through freedom from rashness and boasting. Distinct from existing poverty-of-spirit and peacemaking explanations; no claim that gentleness guarantees prosperity.
- 13:29: the danger of uprooting wheat when trying to remove weeds; distinguishes Jesus' explanation of the field as the world from the Catechism's application to the Church. Existing 7:1 addresses uncertain motives and premature judgements; this note focuses on the mixed field and destructive purification.
- Omitted the drafted 18:15 note after independent letters review identified the same CCC 1829 insight in its restoring-a-brother connection. Letters is adding Matthew 18:15 as an endpoint and incorporating the private-first approach there. DEEP-MAT-003 is intentionally absent; other Matthew IDs remain stable.
- 19:12: freely received celibacy alongside the goodness of marriage. No self-injury reading and no ranking of every unmarried life above marriage.
- 28:17: Matthew's acknowledgement of hesitation among the resurrection witnesses, alongside worship and commissioning. Distinct from the prayer of the father who asks for help with unbelief.

Did not repeat the already developed Beatitudes' general purpose, law/heart teaching, mercy, money, treasure, rock/keys, marriage's permanence, cross or the usual fulfilment texts. Matthew 6:6–7 remains reserved for the root's Teresa/prayer work. The Luke 14/Matthew 10 family connection explains a difficult saying using a parallel and the Corban passage, rather than adding another general note about costly discipleship.

### Mark — three notes

- 9:43: Chrysostom's explicitly figurative reading of severing a limb, from his treatment of the Matthew parallel. The citation is labelled as Matthew; no pretence that he is commenting directly on Mark.
- 10:21: Jesus' love precedes the difficult demand to the rich man; the note centres on that narrative detail rather than repeating detachment in general.
- 14:6: Chrysostom's advice to encourage an imperfect good instead of extinguishing emerging generosity. Uses his exposition of Matthew's parallel, while keeping Mark's burial context and concern for the poor.

Mark's existing notes already cover the kingdom, repentance, prayer, Sabbath, healing, Corban, defilement, the cross, ransom, children, widow and Gethsemane. Added a link between Mark 9 and Eldad/Medad: the particular resemblance is jealous restriction of another person's genuine gift. This differs from the existing Joel/Pentecost link about gifts across age, sex and status, though the wider theology is related. Mark 13:32 is included in the human-knowledge connection, not another end-times timetable explanation.

### Luke — five notes

- 6:20: the direct address to materially poor people, preventing automatic absorption into Matthew's inward wording. Does not romanticise deprivation.
- 12:49: the Catechism's Spirit/fire reading, held beside Jesus' immediately following Passion image.
- 18:7: persistence before God contrasted with the unjust judge, rather than a technique for wearing God down.
- 19:41: the tears that govern the warning to Jerusalem; no collective rejection or delight in the city's suffering.
- 24:26: the apparent necessity of the Passion understood with human freedom, rather than fatalism. The existing Emmaus note explains the heart's awakening through Scripture; this addition addresses the difficult phrase “have to”.

Omitted a drafted Luke 22:32 note after comparison with existing John 21:17 and the keys/shepherding connection: restoration into humble responsibility was already sufficiently developed. No new Good Samaritan/2 Chronicles link, Elijah forty-days link or honouring-vulnerable link, as those are owned by other groups. Luke 10:38–42 remains available to the root's mystical-source pass.

### John — five notes

- 6:44: Augustine's account of being drawn through love and delight. The distinctive point is the education and attraction of desire, not merely the statement that faith cannot be coerced. Flagged a related Hosea 11 freedom note for cross-review.
- 8:11: Augustine on mercy between presumption and despair; Christ opposes sin without abandoning the person.
- 16:7: the advantage of Jesus' departure located in the saving glorification whose fruit the Spirit communicates. Tightened to avoid repeating the generic assurance of presence already at 14:16.
- 16:13: complete revelation in Christ alongside growing understanding over centuries. Following letters' independent review, removed the private-revelation discussion already in its discernment connection and added the directly verified CCC 94 account of contemplation, study, spiritual experience and apostolic preaching. This is distinct from the existing apostolic-discernment connection, which explains the Council's procedure and the Magisterium's service.
- 20:17: Augustine's expressly spiritual reading of touching as believing in the Son's unity with the Father; clearly attributed as one interpretation. His rejection of a gender-based prohibition is preserved.

Rejected Cana/hour and John 3:17 proposals because the existing Cana/intercession and John 3:16 explanations already contain those insights. Did not add another divine-name, bread, side/Adam, shepherd, vine, Thomas, Peter or Trinity note. John 14:23 and 15:4–5 remain reserved for the root. Existing “bronze-serpent-and-cross” already mentions Hezekiah's destruction of the idolised sign; alerted root before another group adds the same connection.

### Acts — eight notes

- 8:20: simony as trying to own a gift whose source remains God.
- 10:47: the Spirit precedes water here, yet Peter still commands baptism; God's freedom and sacramental obedience held together.
- 13:46: this local conflict is not a cancellation of Israel; narrative checks include 13:43 and 14:1.
- 14:15: witnesses refuse deification and direct worship to the Creator.
- 18:26: Priscilla and Aquila's married lay catechesis. Revised following root review to foreground the couple and their role in transmitting faith, leaving the generic teachability theme to wisdom's Proverbs 9:9 note.
- 19:19: converts voluntarily renounce their own magical resources and their financial value; no mandate to destroy other people's books.
- 20:28: the divine person is the subject of genuinely human suffering. Does not say the divine nature itself becomes flesh or dies.
- 27:31: God's promised rescue includes practical means, rather than cancelling the need for the sailors' action.

Omitted Acts 24:16 following root's cross-group review: conscience formed through Scripture, prayer and advice repeats the letters group's Romans 12:2 proposal. Kept established Acts identifiers, so DEEP-ACT-008 is intentionally absent. Did not duplicate Berean/guided Scripture reading, Pentecost/Babel, communal possessions, Stephen's forgiveness, Gentile welcome generally, Council discernment or Paul's engagement with pagan poets.

## New connections

1. `deep-gifts-beyond-our-circle`: Numbers 11:26–29 / Mark 9:38–40. Narrative echo; no assertion of an explicit quotation.
2. `deep-finger-spirit-writing`: Exodus 31:18 / Luke 11:20 / Matthew 12:28 / 2 Corinthians 3:3. CCC 700 explicitly supplies this network. The distinctive bridge is “finger” and “Spirit”, not merely another new-heart reflection.
3. `deep-family-first-allegiance`: Luke 14:26 / Matthew 10:37 / Mark 7:10–13. Reads the actual WEB wording “disregard” with the parallel and the continuing duty to parents. No unsourced Greek claim.
4. `deep-jesus-human-knowing`: Luke 2:52 / Mark 13:32. CCC 472–474 supplies the careful human-knowledge/divine-person distinction, including its interpretation of the unknown hour. Neither a disguise of humanity nor a split into two persons.
5. `deep-hands-gift-spirit`: Acts 8:14–17 / Acts 19:5–6 / Hebrews 6:1–2. CCC 1288 expressly identifies the apostolic imposition of hands as Confirmation's origin. Avoids projecting every later ceremonial detail into Acts or imposing one fixed visible order on all the Spirit's action.

## Source cautions and limits

- Chrysostom's Matthew 80 homily identifies the anointing woman with a sinful woman. That identification is not imported into Mark. Only the explicitly argued pastoral lesson is used.
- Augustine's John 121 offers more than one spiritual reading. The note does not claim its chosen application is the sole literal meaning or make his conjectures about Mary's state of belief into historical facts.
- John's adultery narrative is discussed in its received canonical form; no unsourced manuscript-history claim is introduced.
- These additions make no new Hebrew or Greek lexical claims. The finger/Spirit comparison is a cited intertextual reading, not a proposed etymology.
- No CCEL source is cited in this group; root owns the verified mystical sources and the reserved prayer anchors.
- The five connection evidence entries include exact cached excerpts, raw-HTML hashes and support statements. Their explanations identify theme/echo rather than upgrading every resemblance into a direct fulfilment.
