## Source and interpretation decisions

The Catechism pages were read in the cached Vatican text, including newly fetched sections for Amen, emotions, Holy Orders and the assembly. Exact supporting excerpts are retained with each note. The six connections have matching excerpts and cached raw-HTML SHA-256 evidence.

Three primary patristic pages add distinct exegetical depth:

- Chrysostom, *Homilies on Hebrews* 9.5: the explicit question whether repentance is excluded, followed by the distinction from baptismal renewal. The note presents this as his interpretation, not as a claim that every scholarly difficulty has disappeared.
- Augustine, *Homilies on First John* 2.11–12: created goods, attachment and the different uses of world. The linked explanation does not import his dated examples as contemporary doctrine.
- Chrysostom, *Homilies on Second Timothy* 4.1: chains bind the messenger without binding God's word. The application avoids making suffering harmless or visible missionary success a duty imposed on victims.

The difficult 1 Peter passage is deliberately qualified. The Catechism provides the Catholic doctrinal horizon; it does not establish one uncontested identification of every spirit in 3:19. The new note distinguishes the abode of the dead from final damnation and does not claim universal salvation.

No new Greek or Hebrew claim depends on memory or an unverified etymology. Amen's significance is supported by the Catechism, but the note avoids a separate linguistic excursus. Root owns the mystical-primary-source additions; this group supplies complementary doctrinal and practical discernment.
