# Independent review of the wisdom group

Reviewer: Gospel/Acts agent. Reviewed the 20 proposed note bodies, five connection summaries, source locators and evidence, the wisdom audit, actual cited Bible passages, nearby existing notes and relevant cross-group proposals. Independently opened the cached source contexts for every cited Catechism passage and all six New Advent patristic/Encyclopedia pages. This is an editorial/source review, not a repetition of the author's implementation checks.

## Result

No factual, doctrinal, original-language or source-attribution correction is required in the version reviewed. Two novelty refinements are recommended below. The prose consistently distinguishes a biblical setting from a later Christian application and does not import harmful or dated claims from the older sources.

## Concrete editorial refinements

1. **DEEP-SIR-003, Sirach 37:15:** retain the passage, but remove the generic sentence listing formed conscience, prayer, Scripture, advice and Church teaching. That list and its explanation substantially repeat the new letters note at Romans 12:2 (DEEP-ROM-001, CCC 1784–1785). This Sirach note has its own stronger contribution: testing a counsellor's interests, seeking someone who knows and stands by us, considering the heart, then praying. I sent the author this recommendation; the source itself is fully supportive.
2. **DEEP-PSA-004, Psalm 145:16:** root should consider omitting it for novelty. Its main practical insight—that divine provision/daily bread entails responsibility to share with hungry neighbours—is already explicit in production Matthew 6:11 and 6:33. The image of the open hand and the Catechism's prayer association are sound, but do not introduce much additional content. This is an editorial overlap judgement, not a source problem. If retained, its distinctive value should be the Psalm's place in daily-bread prayer, rather than another broad sharing exhortation.

## Source checks of special importance

- **Job 4:7, CE Job:** the article explicitly distinguishes the inspiration of the whole book from unqualified divine approval of every disputant's claim. The note does not import the article's speculative history, dating or etymology. The current Bible's prologue and later judgement of the friends support the immediate reading.
- **Psalm 56, Augustine §13:** the passage actually speaks of tears placed before God's sight and merciful hearing. The note does not borrow the preceding section's physical-discipline imagery. Page-title numbering and section locator are usable despite the older numbering in Augustine's internal verse references.
- **Psalm 88, Augustine §§2–3:** Christ's human prayer and the Gethsemane sorrow are explicit, as is the distinction between human suffering and sin. The note correctly identifies this as Augustine's Christian reading without erasing the psalm's own voice.
- **Ecclesiastes 7, Gregory Thaumaturgus:** the source directly joins modesty about apparent righteousness/wisdom to taking hold of God and sinning in nothing; it also directly describes the chain of retaliation triggered by overhearing insults. Both notes stay within these claims. The note on “overly righteous” appropriately acknowledges interpretive difficulty rather than treating the paraphrase as modern critical consensus. The source's unrelated misogynistic passage is not used.
- **Psalm 69, Augustine §12:** both New Testament uses of Psalm 69:9 are expressly discussed. Actual John 2:17 and Romans 15:3 quote the two halves. The note's application to zealous service and bearing burdens is warranted; the old exposition's anti-Jewish generalisations are not imported.
- **Psalm 102, Augustine §31:** the passage explicitly links the unending years, the immortal Lord's real death, resurrection and hope. Hebrews 1:10–12 genuinely quotes Psalm 102:25–27 in the Son's context. The summary distinguishes the New Testament quotation from Augustine's related spiritual exposition.
- **Psalm 40/Hebrews 10:** the actual current verses differ as stated (“opened my ears” / “prepared a body”). CCC 462 quotes Hebrews in explaining the Incarnation. No unsupported Hebrew/Greek textual-history account has slipped into the summary.
- **Wisdom 9:15:** CCC 364–365 directly supports body–soul unity, bodily dignity and rejection of contempt for bodily life. The note reads mortal corruption rather than embodiment itself as the problem.
- **Sirach 30:21:** the actual current verse supplies the distinction between sorrow and deliberately afflicting oneself. CCC 1501/2288 supports the ordinary pastoral application; the note does not make depressive illness a moral failure or claim a medical treatment from the ancient text.
- **Other Catechism sources:** the cited paragraphs genuinely support justice as giving another their due, conscience's lifelong formation, work's human purpose, favourable interpretation before condemnation, temperance, the difference between revenge and just correction, works of mercy, prudent particular judgement and the heart's moral formation.

All cited passage ranges were read in the current Bible and all five connection hashes/excerpts matched the cached source evidence.

They are related but do not simply restate the same explanation.
