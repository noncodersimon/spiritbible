# Prophets: deeper editorial review

Draft only. No production files, commits or deployments changed. Final proposed set: **25 notes and six passage connections**. Notes contain 127–138 prose words; all sources remain on the Vatican or New Advent domains. No numerical quota was used.

## Scope and method

Reviewed the existing note catalogue and relevant commentary prose for all 18 assigned books, the existing 78-connection catalogue and relevant links, and the actual WEB wording around candidate passages. The review selects additional interpretive value; it is not a claim to have exhausted every verse or historical question. Source passages were read in cached page context, with precise excerpts retained in the JSON. The Catechism supports doctrinal and spiritual applications; the biblical passage supplies its own immediate literary observations. Encyclopedia material is used for narrative/prophetic context, not dated scholarly conclusions. No new etymology, speculative chronology or invented mystical attribution is included. The root agent handles primary mystical texts separately.

## Book-by-book decisions

| Book | Added | Editorial decision |
|---|---|---|
| Isaiah | 7 notes; heavens/Jordan link | Moral language reversed (5:20); received agency rather than autonomous pride (10:15); hidden yet saving God (45:15); listening before consolation (50:4); the context of freely pardoning behind higher thoughts (55:8); fruitful rather than magical word (55:11); the high God dwelling with the humble (57:15). The final link reads 64:1 beside Mark 1 as an echo, explicitly not a direct quotation. Existing servant, Immanuel, peace, maternal tenderness, fasting and new-creation notes remain sufficient. |
| Jeremiah | 2 notes; Rachel link | Superficial peace that leaves a wound untreated (8:11) and the protective rags in Ebed-melech's rescue (38:12). Rachel's lament is linked to Matthew's explicit reuse, preserving the promise of return and the reality of grief. Rejected another self-knowledge note at 17:10 because 17:9 already makes that point; rejected another general complaint-prayer note at 12:1 because Jeremiah 20 and Habakkuk already cover it. |
| Lamentations | 1 note; inherited-consequences link | Invoking the name from the pit (3:55) supports a clearly marked later Christian application to the Jesus prayer. The apparent tension between 5:7 and Ezekiel 18 clarifies shared consequences versus personal guilt. Existing lament, grace, waiting and non-victim-blaming repentance are not repeated. |
| Baruch | 1 note | 4:28's tenfold seeking means wholehearted return, not an arithmetic exchange rate buying forgiveness. Existing wisdom/Torah, incarnational reception, exile and idolatry notes already cover the major horizons. |
| Ezekiel | 3 notes; inherited-consequences link | Sodom's neglected poor (16:49), hallowing the name without divine vanity (36:22), and the joined sticks as concrete reconciliation of Israel/Judah (37:17). Rejected 33:12 because the existing 18:23 note already explains past virtue/past failure and present conversion. Existing shepherd, heart, breath and river links are retained without parallel duplicates. |
| Daniel | 2 notes | Reading the promise becomes prayer rather than passive prediction (9:2); lived purification makes understanding possible (12:10), using the Catechism's Augustine citation. No extra end-times calendar, fourth-figure identification, martyrdom or Susanna note: existing coverage handles these carefully. |
| Hosea | 1 note | Husband/master address (2:16) is developed as the growth from fear or wages towards loving fidelity; the following removal of Baal names and justice/compassion context remain explicit. Dropped 11:4 after independent review identified the new Gospel John 6:44 note's more fitting treatment of attraction and freedom, plus existing Hosea parental-care coverage. No repeated mercy-not-sacrifice or Egypt-son link. |
| Joel | 1 note | 3:14's valley of decision is the setting of God's judgement, clarified from 3:12–13 rather than a crowd deciding whether to believe. No repeated Pentecost, fasting or locust-restoration note. |
| Amos | No addition retained | Considered a commercial-fraud note at 8:5. Independent review identified that Proverbs 11:1 already develops the same scales/contracts/CCC2409 insight, and Amos 2:6 already questions how wealth is acquired. Removed the draft rather than add volume. Election/accountability, durable justice and hearing the word are already covered. |
| Obadiah | No addition | Considered bystander complicity at 1:11; existing 1:12 already treats morally serious spectatorship and practical mercy. Pride and the kingdom's final belonging to God are also covered. No forced short-book addition. |
| Jonah | 1 note | The sailors' strenuous attempt to save Jonah (1:13) makes outsiders' practical respect for life visible before the prophet's later refusal of mercy. This is a literary/ethical observation with Catholic charity application, not a claim endorsing every sailor's belief. Existing descent/resurrection, conditional prophecy and Nineveh mercy remain distinct. |
| Micah | 1 note | 3:8's prophetic power is tested by justice and truth against paid reassurance (3:5), with the Catechism's discernment and charity standard for gifts. Existing land exploitation, peace, Bethlehem and the triad of justice/mercy/humility remain adequate. |
| Nahum | No addition | Considered the bloody city at 3:1, but existing 1:3 already treats imperial cruelty, judgement without revenge and care for victims. Refuge and restored communal life also covered. Avoided multiplying violent-oracle applications. |
| Habakkuk | 1 note | 1:13 sharpens the second complaint: historical usefulness or success does not morally justify treachery. CCC311 supports providence without God authoring moral evil. Independent review separated this from Isaiah 10 by rewriting the Isaiah note around real received agency. Existing initial protest, waiting, faith and joy amid loss remain distinct. |
| Zephaniah | 1 connection | 3:19–20's honour for those previously shamed links to the honour of apparently weaker members in 1 Corinthians 12 and guests unable to repay in Luke 14. This concerns concrete participation and regard, distinct from existing remnant humility and divine rejoicing. No separate note repeating the link. |
| Haggai | 1 note | 2:14's priestly questions distinguish holiness from proximity to sacred things. Christian application carefully preserves sacramental efficacy through Christ and receptive disposition (CCC1128), avoiding the claim that a minister's sin invalidates a sacrament. Existing rebuilding priorities, humble work and peace cover other main candidates. |
| Zechariah | 1 note; 2 connections | 8:23 preserves the nations' gratitude towards Jewish Israel rather than replacement contempt. Joshua's accusation connects to Romans 8 and Revelation 12; the struck shepherd connects to Jesus' explicit citation and promised Galilee reunion. Existing 3:4 already explains cleansing, so its new linked treatment focuses on accusation versus Christ's intercession. No repeat of donkey, pierced figure, oil or ordinary holiness. |
| Malachi | 2 notes | 2:14's marital covenant includes the Catechism's distinction between betrayal and innocent abandonment, with legitimate separation acknowledged. 3:10 retains agricultural/storehouse/covenantal context and rejects a universal cash-return promise. Existing pure offering, teaching responsibility, refining, remembrance and Elijah links remain sufficient. |

## Strongest additions and safeguards

The hidden God who is still Saviour, protective rags in Jeremiah's rescue, Sodom's ignored poor, and the valley of decision are especially passage-specific. The linked sets supply significant connections without repeatedly revisiting the existing servant, new-covenant or Spirit outlines. Israel's original context remains explicit; quotation, echo and thematic comparison are distinguished. No collective Jewish guilt, victim-blaming account of disability, coercive marriage instruction or claim that guaranteed wealth follows donations is introduced.
