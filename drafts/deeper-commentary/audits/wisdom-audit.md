# Wisdom books: deeper editorial audit

Reviewed all existing notes and connection endpoints for Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom and Sirach against the exact current WEB wording. The final proposal contains 19 new notes and five connections, selected for gaps rather than distributed to meet a quota. No production files changed.

## Book-by-book decisions

- **Job** (20 existing notes): existing coverage already handles innocent suffering, presence beside grief, simplistic prosperity theology, mediation, creation's scale and Job's final encounter. Three additions distinguish an inspired dialogue from the truth of every speaker's assertion (4:7), being known by God while unable to find him (23:10), and the investigative attention required by justice towards strangers (29:16). Avoided another generic note about trusting through suffering; the 23:10 note explicitly separates trust from a claimed explanation of affliction.
- **Psalms** (55 existing notes and several major Christological connections): added tears held before God (56:8), a lament that actually ends in darkness (88:18), personal learning from affliction without universalising its value (119:71), and authority measured by protected rights (82:3). Added three precise New Testament quotation connections: Psalm40/Hebrews10; both halves of Psalm69:9 in John2 and Romans15; Psalm102 in Hebrews1. Existing Psalm22, Psalm16, Psalm110 and rejected-stone connections were not repeated. Psalm131 was reserved for root's mystical review and then omitted there as already sufficiently covered. Psalm44/Romans8 remains a possible future source-backed addition, not an unfinished required item.
- **Proverbs** (22 existing notes, no existing connections): focused on teachability (9:9), the unavoidable demands of fruitful activity (14:4), procedural fairness in hearing a case (18:17), temperance as receiving good things well (25:16), and strength as command of anger (16:32). These are more specific than the existing general prudence, conscience and gentle-answer notes. A shared connection explains the opposite instructions in 26:4–5 through prudence, Sirach20 and Ecclesiastes3. Omitted a correction/faithful-wounds connection because the letters group is developing the stronger Galatians6/2Thessalonians3/James5 connection. Omitted another hidden-motives note because Proverbs20:27 already covers conscience.
- **Ecclesiastes** (12 existing notes): added work governed by enough (4:6), careful Christian reception of the difficult 'overly righteous' sentence (7:16), and freedom from collecting every disparaging remark (7:21). The last is about forbearance rooted in shared fallibility, distinct from Proverbs18:17's fairness before judgement. Existing enjoyment-as-gift, mortality, seasons, companionship and uncertainty/action notes already handle the obvious expansion points. Ecclesiastes3:7 participates in the prudence connection without repeating its chapter's note on seasons.
- **Song of Songs** (12 existing notes and love-strong-as-death connection): existing annotations already distinguish human love and Christian spiritual reception, consent, desire, absence, interiority, mutual belonging and love's unpurchasable quality. Root reserved Song5 for primary mystical-source review and later rejected duplication. I have not added a generic mystical reading simply to give this book a new entry. A future passage-specific commentary from a verified primary exposition could justify an addition.
- **Wisdom** (22 existing notes and righteous-son connection): added the important clarification of 9:15: the mortal body's limits do not mean that bodily existence is evil. The source anchors this explicitly in Catholic body–soul unity and bodily dignity. Omitted 8:21 prayer for wisdom because 7:7 and 9:17 already cover divine gift; omitted early death because 4:8 already treats maturity beyond years; omitted gradual correction because 12:18 already explains divine forbearance; omitted generic immortality/creation material already amply represented.
- **Sirach** (27 existing notes and forgiveness connection): added the relational harm of humiliating a gift's recipient (18:15), careful reception of advice against deliberately worsening sorrow without blaming grief or depressive illness (30:21), and the testing of advisers’ interests, character and willingness to grieve with us if we fail (37:15). Two connections develop silence chosen at the right time (20:5–7) and speech as fruit of inward cultivation (27:6–7/Luke6:43–45). The latter is labelled an echo/shared wisdom image, not asserted direct literary borrowing. Omitted Sirach38 medicine because history owns that connection; omitted craft as prayer because existing38:24 already explicitly covers it; omitted generic friendship/mercy/remembering saints already covered at6:14,18:13 and44:1.

## Source and interpretive checks

Sources are actual cached Vatican Catechism pages and New Advent primary Fathers/Encyclopedia pages. Every source excerpt was matched against whitespace-normalised cached text; every connection includes its source HTML SHA256 evidence. Augustine's webpage titles use the modern psalm number while internal verse references often use the older numbering; citations use the webpage title consistently and give section numbers. No invented language etymologies were added.

The Job article is used only for the distinction between the whole inspired dialogue and its disputants' claims, not its dated historical hypotheses. Gregory Thaumaturgus is identified as an early Christian paraphrast for Ecclesiastes7, and his handling is presented as a Christian reception rather than a claim to settle every historical-critical question. His unrelated dated statements about women are not used. Augustine's Psalm69 passage is used for the actual paired New Testament quotations without importing its polemical generalisations about Jews.

The Psalms102/Hebrews1 link's New Testament quotation is checked directly against both Bible passages; Augustine supports the associated Christian hope in the immortal Lord entering mortality. The Psalm40/Hebrews10 link notes differing wording but makes no unsupported claim about the textual history of Hebrew and Greek versions. Practical applications are distinguished from the claims the source explicitly supports.
